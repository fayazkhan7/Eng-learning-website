Present perfect vs. past simple: which tense do you need?

Both tenses describe the past. The difference is whether that past is still connected to now — and that decides the verb you need.

Most learners know the forms. The hard part is choosing between them while speaking, when there is no time to think about rules. The choice comes down to one question: is the time period finished, or does it still include now?

When the time is finished, use the past simple

If you can name a finished time — yesterday, last year, in 2019, ten minutes ago — the period is closed. Use the past simple.

  • I watched that lecture last night.
  • She moved to Dubai in 2021.
  • We didn't finish the exercise yesterday.

When the time is unfinished, use the present perfect

The present perfect connects a past action to the present. That might mean the action happened at some point in a period that is still open, or that its result matters now.

  • I have watched that lecture. (at some point — and I know it now)
  • She has moved house three times. (in her life so far)
  • We haven't finished the exercise yet. (still possible today)
If you can add "…and that matters now", you probably need the present perfect. If you can add "…and that time is over", you probably need the past simple.

The two sentences that show the difference

Compare these:

  • I lost my keys. (and then I found them — the story is finished)
  • I have lost my keys. (so I cannot get in — the result is present)

The facts are almost the same. What changes is where the speaker puts the focus: on the event, or on its effect now.

Common mistakes

  • Using the present perfect with a finished time. "I have seen him yesterday" is wrong; use "I saw him yesterday".
  • Using the past simple for life experience. "Did you ever eat sushi?" is understandable, but "Have you ever eaten sushi?" is the standard form.
  • Forgetting the irregular forms. go → went → gone, see → saw → seen. The participle is what the present perfect needs.

A quick test

Choose the correct form, then check the reasoning:

  1. I ______ (finish) the report — here it is. → have finished: the result is present.
  2. I ______ (finish) the report at 9 p.m. last night. → finished: the time is finished.
  3. She ______ (live) in Lahore for six years. → has lived: she still does.
Practice tip: When you read in English, highlight every present perfect you find and ask what it connects to in the present. That question — "why now?" — is the skill the tense is built around.
